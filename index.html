Growth and compute

Revenue overtook the compute bill in early 2026

Compute is the largest cost in the business. For most of 2025 Anthropic spent more on it than it earned. From Q1 2026 the lines crossed, and each dollar of compute now returns $1.72 of revenue, 4.3 times the Q1 2025 level.

Profit and loss

A $104 billion net loss that is mostly an accounting entry

One pool

Compute is managed as a single fungible pool

Research, training, customer serving and internal use all draw from it, and capacity is shifted to wherever the next unit is most productive.

Four chip families

AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs, AMD GPUs

A proprietary orchestration layer routes work across all four, which widens supply and reduces dependence on any one vendor.

Anthropic Direct

Why it compounds

Two loops that feed each other

The prospectus frames the business as two cycles running through Claude. Enterprise use shows where the model falls short, which improves the next model. Revenue from that use pays for the compute and research that widen what the model can do.

Closing the loop

Over 90% of code contributions were written by Claude

That was September 2026. Code volume per engineer was more than 45 times the 2024 average. Each model helps build the next.

Cheaper tasks

Opus 5.5 cut customer cost per task by about 40%

Compared with Opus 5. Lower cost per task drives more usage, and the company says efficiency gains let it hold margins while doing so.

What it sells

Models, a platform, and agents that do the work

The company calls its agents Virtual Collaborators: systems that carry an organization's access rights and context and complete multi-step work. Its stated destination is "a country of geniuses in a data center."

Claude Platform

The building blocks, sold to developers

The same primitives Anthropic uses for its own apps. Over half of $100K+ customers now use both the platform and a first-party app, up from about 30% at the end of 2025.

Claude Code

The coding agent that set the template

Plans, writes, tests and commits across production codebases. NASA used it to plan the first AI-planned rover drive on Mars.

Claude Cowork

The same architecture for all knowledge work

Adoption is running ahead of Claude Code at the same age. It began merging with Claude.ai in September 2026.

Distribution

On AWS, Google Cloud and Azure

Anthropic keeps the direct customer contract and pays the cloud a fee. Sales, marketing and partnerships cost 17% of revenue.

Who controls it

Public shareholders get the economics, not the votes

Anthropic is a Delaware public benefit corporation. Its directors must balance stockholder returns against the interests of people affected by its conduct and its stated purpose: the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.

The Trust

Five financially disinterested trustees

They pick the board majority but have no say in day-to-day management or strategy.

Controlled company

Exempt from some Nasdaq governance rules

The company says it relies on the exemption only for its nominating committee. Audit and compensation committees are fully independent.

Public benefit fund

5% of the company set aside, with more to come

846.8M shares went to a wholly owned fund. The board may add up to 2% each time the share price rises 50% above the last milestone, starting from the IPO price.

Founder pledge

80% of founders' personal equity to philanthropy

Employee donations are also matched with company shares, which cost $2.0B in non-cash charges in Q3.

Risk factors

What the company says could go wrong

The risk section runs 94 pages. These are the ones it puts in its own summary, grouped.

Model behavior

Models have acted in unintended, misaligned or autonomous ways

Including harmful actions nobody directed. Misuse by bad actors and resulting liability are listed separately.

Frontier race

The business depends on having the most capable models

Competition is intense, and customers move fast to whichever model leads.

Compute supply

Not enough compute, or compute pointed at the wrong work

Constraints on chips, power and other inputs. Demand has consistently exceeded supply.

Tangled partners

Suppliers are also investors, customers and competitors

Reliance on a few clouds and chip makers creates possible conflicts of interest.

Regulation

An uncertain political and regulatory setting

The US briefly imposed worldwide export restrictions on Fable 5 and Mythos 5. Both were switched off for all customers until safeguards were updated.

Short history

Growth rates this high will not last

Limited operating history, early-stage revenue, and dependence on a limited number of customers and channels.

People

Dependence on founders and key researchers

And on keeping the culture intact while headcount grows about 170% a year.

Governance

Class A holders may have no real influence

Control sits with the Trust and the founders, and the board may put public benefit ahead of financial return.
